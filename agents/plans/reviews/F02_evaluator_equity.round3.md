ROUND 3 REVIEW of `agents/plans/F02_evaluator_equity.md`

B1 [major] §3.3 river phases 1–4 / §8.2 unbiasedness guarantee — The same phase-1 samples supply both the fallback estimate and the measured cost that decides whether to replace it with exact equity. Evaluation cost can depend on the sampled hand, so this selection can correlate with the sample values. Returning either an exact value or an individually unbiased sample mean does not make their adaptive selection unbiased. A counterexample using the stated constants: two equally weighted opponent pairs have showdown values 1 and 0, costing respectively 1 and 2 fake-time units per evaluation; budget 96 units, negligible preparation and hero-evaluation cost. If the first 64 samples contain K wins, they cost 128−K units. The upgrade criterion selects exact equity 0.5 for K≥37. For K≤32, the expired deadline returns K/64. For 33≤K≤36, another 32 samples are taken, whose conditional expected estimate is (K+16)/96. Summing over K~Binomial(64,0.5) gives expected returned equity 0.4828765077, while true equity is 0.5. — Separate calibration/selection work from the samples used in a returned estimate, and specify a sampling/selection rule that supports the unbiasedness claim. A mean stopped by outcome-dependent elapsed time also needs treatment. Alternatively, explicitly adopt an approximate adaptive estimator and quantify its bias instead of guaranteeing unbiasedness. Add a fake-evaluator test whose cost depends on the sampled hand, checking the distribution over seeds. The two round 2 findings are otherwise addressed.
